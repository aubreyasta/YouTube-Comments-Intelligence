What campaigns done by Nike are most successful?
You've analysed two Nike campaigns. Bola Rakyat is the stronger of the two by transfer: 41% of its 8,412 comments echoed at least one idea from the brief, against 26% for Lari Pagi. The gap is almost entirely cultural: the "every kampung is a stadium" line carried on its own at 47%, while Lari Pagi's strongest idea only reached 19%.
Both films share the same weakness — the product claim didn't travel. Concrete durability got 14% on Bola Rakyat and 11% on Lari Pagi, and in both cases the tone turned into a question rather than a belief.

Do people value price over luxury when it comes to coffee?
In the one coffee campaign you've run, price wins clearly. Price-and-value talk took 31% of 4,106 comments — the largest theme — while craft, origin and ritual together reached 17%. Where the two appear in the same comment, price is usually the objection and luxury the thing being dismissed.
Worth noting the brief never raised price, so this came up unprompted.

What we heard
The film's cultural claim landed almost intact. Nearly half of the conversation repeated the idea that the neighbourhood pitch is the real stadium, often unprompted and in the audience's own words — the strongest transfer we have measured on this account.
"lapangan depan rumah gue lebih rame dari GBK kalau sore 😂"Nostalgia · joy · 1,204 likes
The product did not travel with it. Only 14% engaged the concrete-boot claim, and where they did, the tone flipped to a question: how long will these last on a real street pitch? Price talk took 18% of the conversation without the brief inviting it, which suggests the "for everyone" line was heard as a promise about cost, not access.
One caution. Sarcasm is the label to trust least — Indonesian comment humour reads as praise to the model more often than we would like. Treat the "joy" share on the doubt theme as a floor, not a ceiling.
